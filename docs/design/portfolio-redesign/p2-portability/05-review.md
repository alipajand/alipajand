## Defects found and fixed in P2.1

Found in stills and recorded frames, not by the automated checks:

1. **EM-02 station labels overlapped their sub-labels** ("Input to the module" wrapped into "Data or interaction"). The boxes are taller and the labels wrap earlier.
2. **The AG chain faded while its card was still on screen.** The match cut began at the landing beat's first frame. Both scenes now hold their last drawing for the first 12% of the landing span and fade it by 24%, so the card is mostly read first.
3. **The surface drop lines dangled at the top of the frame** after the AG camera moved down. They now fade with the surfaces.
4. **Cut-band tags were truncated** ("Shipped screensh…"). The tags are shorter, the split favours the material above the cut, and below 1,360 px only the sheet code shows; the brief names the material in full.
5. **The left A bubble covered the "The interface," datum**, and the dash-dot line ran through the datum text. The datums now sit clear of the bubble, wholly on their own side of the cut.
6. **In the condensed row, the cut ran through the key column's text.** The key-column end of the cut and the left bubble fade with the datums.
7. **AlwaysGeeky's long name pushed "Case study →" onto its own line**, so its title block did not match the other two. Every bay now puts the cue under the name; on phones each name reserves two lines so the three cues align.
8. **A brief lowercased "React" and "D3".** Fixed at the source.

## Recordings: what was reviewed

Each recording was reviewed as video and as frame grids sampled across the clip.

- **Desktop, `desktop-full.mp4` (56 s).** **Index:** the first frame shows the section with all three bays, the cut, the datums and the title block. Over a three-second scroll the identity block slides up under the bar, then the bays close toward the cut; the left A bubble and the key-column end of the cut fade, and the briefs rise attached to the condensed row with no gap or overlap in any sampled frame. Pointing at each bay quiets the other two. Supporting work and the experiment follow, then a discipline mark. **AlwaysGeeky:** callouts, lens, assembly, then one camera move down the blue line; AG-03's spine, clauses and gates are drawn before the card reaches the reading position and hold while it is read. The chain then fades and the marketplace screenshot lands in the hero, left of the heading. **Emplifi:** cursor, then the phone steps aside for the render path (stations and probe draw in order), composition, then the frame budget with its stamp. EM-04 holds while its card is read; the phone returns and lands left of the heading. **In no sampled frame does either subject cross the case-study heading.**
- **Phone, `mobile-full.mp4` (57 s).** The opening condenses with the legend and timeframes kept; the three cores and their "Case study →" cues stay aligned. Both scenes run in the window above the cards. At the landing, the last drawing fades inside the opaque window first, then the window lets go and the subject lands above the heading. (A first take showed the fading drawing ghosting over the card text for about half a second, because the window and the drawing faded together. The window now fades after the drawing; the take was re-recorded.)
- **Reduced motion, `reduced-motion.mp4` (50 s).** The Motion control reads "Motion off". The index is one static column: no pinning, no condensation. Both scenes are static steps with their figures, followed by the case study.
- **LedgerGuard, `ledgerguard-flat-depth.mp4` (Chromium) and `webkit-ledgerguard-flat-depth.mp4` (headless WebKit), both at the default URL.** The flat drawn section runs in both: open first frame, LG-01 as a plate, LG-02 to LG-05 as bands split by the red line, the workspace prototype with the approved captions, and the dashboard landing in the story's figure. Neither take is a shipping-Safari observation.

## Found by the performance probe, not fixed

The opening condensation causes cumulative layout shift during scroll: 0.117 on desktop and 0.378 at phone width (04). No functional check catches it, because nothing moves out from under the reader. It is listed as a production blocker in 04 and as an open item in 06.
